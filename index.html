VA GLA · General Medicine

Where can this patient safely go?

A starting-point decision aid for discharge planning from acute inpatient General Medicine. Answer a few questions to narrow the field of disposition options and see what to confirm with your social worker or case manager.

This is a guide, not a determination. Final disposition always depends on bed availability, patient/family preference, insurance verification, and the judgment of SW, case management, and the accepting facility. Assumes the patient is medically stable to leave acute medicine.

Based on the VA GLA Transitions of Care Primer
Created by George Tran, MD · Last updated October 2026

Step 1 — Goal of care

Is the goal of care comfort-focused / end-of-life?

If the patient is transitioning to comfort care, the disposition pathway is different and runs through Palliative Care.

Step 2 — Care needs

Does the patient have an ongoing skilled need?

Skilled needs include: IV antibiotics, daily complex wound care, ongoing PT/OT, or first-time tube feeds making up >50% of daily nutrition. This is the biggest fork in the pathway.

Step 3 — Functional status

What is the patient's functional status?

Independence in ADLs/iADLs gates many options. Several housing programs require full independence; facilities differ on transfer ability.

Step 4 — Cognition & safety

Cognitive status and elopement risk?

Severe impairment with elopement risk requires a locked/secure setting and drives DPOA / capacity-declaration requirements.

Step 5 — Housing

Does the patient have safe, stable housing to return to?

This separates "home" pathways from housing-program and facility pathways.

Step — Substance use

Does the patient have an active substance use disorder and are they interested in receiving treatment?

This opens the Domiciliary (DOM) residential treatment program as a disposition option. DOM requires both active SUD and willingness to engage in treatment.

Step — Funding & eligibility

What is the funding situation for a long-term or custodial facility stay?

Custodial (non-skilled, long-term) facility placement is not covered by Medicare. This determines which custodial settings are realistic.

Step — Service connection

What is the patient's VA service connection (SC) rating?

Long-stay custodial placement at the on-campus CLC requires a high service connection rating. Short-stay CLC has no SC requirement.
